# Editorial Rules — The Invisible Why

These rules are binding for every agent in the pipeline. When an agent's
output conflicts with these rules, the rule wins and the output must be
revised or rejected.

## 1. Topic scope

A topic qualifies if it credibly contains **at least two** of:

- a surprising contradiction ("the opposite of what you'd assume")
- a hidden mechanism (psychology, biology, physics, economics)
- clear personal relevance (money, health, decisions, everyday life)
- a strong, sourceable "why," not just a "what"

Topics that are pure trivia, pure news, or require constant hedging to stay
accurate are rejected by the Idea Scorer regardless of curiosity appeal.

## 2. No unverified causal claims

Never say "X causes Y" unless the Research Agent's dossier marks the
causal link as established (not merely correlational or preliminary). When
evidence is mixed, the script must say so in plain language ("researchers
are still debating why").

## 3. No medical, legal, or financial advice

The channel explains mechanisms. It does not tell viewers what to do with
their money, health, or legal situation. Any sentence that reads as
prescriptive advice ("you should therefore...") gets flagged by the Fact
Checker and rewritten as description, not instruction.

## 4. Numbers need a source and a unit

Every statistic in a script must trace to a specific source in the research
dossier, with the correct unit and time frame preserved. "Studies show"
without a citable study is not allowed past the Fact Checker.

## 5. No fabricated studies, quotes, or experts

If a claim would be stronger with a named study/expert and none exists in
the research dossier, the claim is either cut or reframed as a general
mechanism ("researchers have found," with the actual citation in the
description) — never invented.

## 6. Real people and events

No synthetic depiction of real, identifiable people. Illustrations are
stylized line-drawing characters only — no attempt to resemble a specific
real person. If a real event/person must be referenced, it's handled in
narration + on-screen text, not in a "deepfake-adjacent" visual.

## 7. Clickbait ceiling

Titles and thumbnails may create curiosity and tension, but the video must
deliver on the literal claim in the title within the runtime. A title
promising "the hidden reason" must actually reveal a hidden reason, not
restate the obvious one.

## 8. Length and pacing discipline

- Target 9–14 minutes for main uploads (long enough for mid-roll ads,
  short enough to protect retention)
- A new visual beat at least every 20–40 seconds
- A new question / twist / escalation at least every 60–90 seconds
- No content padding solely to cross the 8-minute monetization threshold

## 9. Human sign-off gates (never automated)

- Final topic approval
- Script fact-check sign-off on medical/financial/legal-adjacent topics
- Title + thumbnail selection
- Public visibility (uploads land as **private**; a human publishes)

## 10. Attribution & sourcing in metadata

Every published video's description includes a short "Sources" section
(3–8 links) so claims are independently checkable. This is a non-negotiable
trust signal for the channel and a defense against demonetization review.
