# Source Policy — The Invisible Why

## Source tiering (highest to lowest trust)

1. **Primary research** — peer-reviewed studies, government/regulatory data,
   university publications, original datasets
2. **Institutional secondary** — university press offices, national health
   bodies (NIH, CDC, NHS, etc.), central banks, standards bodies
3. **Quality science/finance journalism** — outlets with a correction policy
   and named authors (used to translate primary sources, not as the primary
   source itself)
4. **Expert commentary** — named, credentialed individuals, clearly
   attributed as opinion/interpretation, not fact
5. **Forums, social posts, "I read somewhere"** — never used as a factual
   basis; may be used only as an *anecdote framing device*, explicitly
   labeled as anecdotal on screen ("one Reddit user described...")

## Rules

- **Money, health, and legal topics require at least one Tier 1 or Tier 2
  source per major claim.** No exceptions.
- Every research dossier lists sources with: title, publisher, year, URL,
  and the exact claim it supports — so the Fact Checker can verify claim-to-
  source mapping sentence by sentence.
- If a claim only has Tier 3–4 support, the script must phrase it as
  reported/contested, not as settled fact.
- A single source is never enough for a surprising or counter-intuitive
  claim — the Research Agent must find at least one corroborating source or
  flag the claim as single-sourced for human review.
- Sources older than 10 years are flagged for a "has this been
  superseded?" check, except historical/foundational material where age is
  expected (e.g., a classic psychology study, correctly framed as such,
  including replication status if known).
- Paywalled sources are fine to *read* but the public-facing citation in
  the video description should link to the most accessible version
  (abstract, press release, or open-access mirror) so viewers can actually
  check it.

## Replication & controversy flag

Psychology in particular has a replication crisis. Any cited study must be
checked (by the Fact Checker) for known replication failures or major
critiques before it's allowed into a script. If a famous-but-shaky study
(e.g., commonly-misused pop-psych studies) comes up in research, the script
either skips it or explicitly names the controversy as part of the story.
