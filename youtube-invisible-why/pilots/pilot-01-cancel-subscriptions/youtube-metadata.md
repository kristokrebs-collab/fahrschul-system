## Title

```
Why Your Brain Refuses to Cancel Subscriptions
```

## Description
```
You signed up in ninety seconds. Cancelling took five screens, a
retention offer, and a phone number you had to call during business
hours. That asymmetry isn't laziness on your part — it's three
well-documented cognitive biases being deliberately engineered against.

This video breaks down exactly which parts of your brain a cancel button
is designed to fight: status quo bias, loss aversion, and the sunk cost
fallacy — plus what large-scale research found when it actually measured
how many subscription services build "Roach Motel" cancellation flows on
purpose.

SOURCES
• Samuelson, W., & Zeckhauser, R. (1988). "Status Quo Bias in Decision
  Making." Journal of Risk and Uncertainty, 1(1), 7–59.
• Kahneman, D., Knetsch, J. L., & Thaler, R. H. (1991). "Anomalies: The
  Endowment Effect, Loss Aversion, and Status Quo Bias." Journal of
  Economic Perspectives, 5(1), 193–206.
• Arkes, H. R., & Blumer, C. (1985). "The Psychology of Sunk Cost."
  Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 35(1), 124–140.

  Patterns at Scale: Findings from a Crawl of 11K Shopping Websites."

This video explains mechanisms. It is not financial advice.
```
