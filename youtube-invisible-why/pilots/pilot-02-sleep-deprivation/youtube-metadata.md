## Description
```
At 3 AM, after a day and a half awake, your brain doesn't warn you. It
feels exactly as confident as it did yesterday afternoon — while making
measurably worse decisions.

This video walks the timeline hour by hour: what's actually happening
inside your skull, why sleep loss hits flexible decisions harder than
routine ones, and why the most dangerous part isn't feeling terrible —
it's that you don't.

Includes the part most videos get wrong: this is NOT "willpower running
out like a battery." That idea didn't survive replication. The real
mechanism is more specific, and more physical.

CHAPTERS
0:00 3 AM, and your brain isn't warning you
0:31 What you'll know by the end
0:47 The hour-by-hour timeline
1:38 Why "you're just slower" misses the point
2:00 The gap between feeling and performance
2:30 Shifting toward riskier choices
3:00 Why it isn't willpower
3:45 Flexible decisions vs. routine ones
4:25 The fix is structural, not motivational
4:57 Sleep first, decide after

SOURCES
• Dawson, D., & Reid, K. (1997). "Fatigue, alcohol and performance
  impairment." Nature, 388(6639), 235.
• Williamson, A. M., & Feyer, A. M. (2000). "Moderate sleep deprivation
  produces impairments in cognitive and motor performance equivalent to
  legally prescribed levels of alcohol intoxication." Occupational and
  Environmental Medicine, 57(10), 649–655.
• Van Dongen, H. P. A., et al. (2003). "The cumulative cost of additional
  wakefulness." Sleep, 26(2), 117–126.
• Venkatraman, V., et al. (2011). "Sleep deprivation biases the neural
  mediation of economic preferences toward risk seeking." Journal of
  Neuroscience, 31(10), 3712–3718.
• Harrison, Y., & Horne, J. A. (2000). "One night of sleep loss impairs
  innovative thinking and flexible decision making." Organizational
  Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 81(1), 128–145.
• Killgore, W. D. S. (2010). "Effects of sleep deprivation on cognition."
  Progress in Brain Research, 185, 105–129.
• Hagger, M. S., et al. (2016). "A Multilab Preregistered Replication of
  the Ego-Depletion Effect." Perspectives on Psychological Science,
  11(4), 546–573 — cited for why this video does NOT use ego depletion.

The alcohol comparison refers to measured task-performance curves, not a
claim that sleep loss and intoxication share a biological mechanism.
Individual tolerance varies. This video explains mechanisms and is not
medical advice.
```
